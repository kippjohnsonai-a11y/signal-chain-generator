    Include
    Which output rig do I pick?

    The rig is where the pedal's sound goes, so the preset knows whether to include an amp.

    • Headphones / FRFR / PA: headphones, a full-range speaker (FRFR) or straight into a mixer. The pedal does the amp and speaker cabinet. Pick this if you're not sure.
    • Front of amp: the pedal plugs into a real guitar amp's input, so it gets effects only, no amp model.
    • 4-cable method: the pedal is wired into your amp's effects loop too. Drives go before the amp, delay and reverb after it.
    • NAM on pedal / NAM on computer: for players using Neural Amp Modeler captures. Leave these alone unless you've set NAM up (More > NAM captures).

    Building a preset needs an AI key (Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini): add one in More > AI. A blank song, imports and the pedal work without one.

    How this works

    Your toggle choices are remembered. With the wah off, the expression pedal becomes a volume pedal. Every preset gets 4 snapshots, one per section of the song. Claude also says whether the song would benefit from the wah, expression pedal, looper, or Poly Capo. On a NAM rig, "Full-rig captures only" sticks to captures that include their cab, which saves the cab block's DSP. The box above the instructions shows how much DSP your rig and toggles leave and which other pedals still fit. The Stage boost is an off drive before the amp that Stage mode's Gain boost switches on.

    Build a preset yourself, block by block, with no AI and no API key needed.

    No AI: you get a preset with 4 snapshots (Intro, Verse, Chorus, Solo) and your usual noise gate. Build it with Edit values. The amp and cab apply to the headphones/FRFR rig; other rigs start empty. You can have Claude write a song plan later if you like.

    Bring in a preset you already have, from a slot on the pedal or from an .hlx file, so you can keep it, tweak it and use it in set lists.

    No AI, and nothing on the pedal changes. From the pedal: quit HX Edit first (only one program can use the USB connection). From a file: export a preset from HX Edit, or use one you downloaded. Blocks, values, snapshots, footswitches, expression pedal, gate and output come across; anything that doesn't is listed in the new version's notes.

    Versions

    Every tweak or save adds a version, and older ones are kept. Tap one to open it. The ★ one opens first.

                  Paired

                    How to use this app

                    Signal Chain Generator makes presets for the Line 6 HX Stomp XL. You type a song, pick your guitar, and it builds the amp and effects for that song, ready to send to the pedal. You can then tweak the sound in plain words, keep set lists, and use big Stage buttons at a gig.

                    Before you start

                    1. Your HX Stomp XL and a USB cable. Use a good, newer cable that carries data. Some cables only charge, and an old cable can let the app read the pedal but make sending presets fail.
                    2. HX Edit installed. HX Edit is Line 6's free editor (line6.com/software). The app reads the pedal's list of amps and effects from it, so it must be installed. Close HX Edit while you use this app: only one program can talk to the pedal at a time.
                    3. An AI key (optional). The AI that builds and tweaks presets runs on your own key: Claude (from console.anthropic.com), ChatGPT (platform.openai.com) or Gemini (aistudio.google.com). You pay that company directly, usually a few cents to a few tens of cents per preset. Without a key you can still import presets, build them by hand, edit, make set lists and send to the pedal. Pick the AI and add the key in More > AI.
                    4. Back up your pedal once. In HX Edit choose File > Backup before your first send. The app also backs up each slot before it overwrites it, but your own backup is the safest.

                    More > Set up this computer checks these for you.

                    1. Add your guitar

                    Open More > Guitars (on a computer, Guitars at the top). Add a name, the pickups and the tuning. Every preset is set up for one guitar, because pickups change the sound a lot: single coils (like a Strat) need different settings than humbuckers (like a Les Paul). Add every guitar you play; you can remake any song for another guitar later.

                    2. Make your first song

                    Tap + New song. There are three ways to start:

                    • Generate with Claude: type the song and artist (say "Back in Black by AC/DC"), pick your guitar and output rig, and tap Generate preset. It takes about a minute. If you're not sure which rig, pick Headphones / FRFR / PA.
                    • Start blank: an empty preset you build yourself. No key needed.
                    • Import a preset: bring in one that's already on your pedal or saved as an .hlx file. No key needed, and nothing on the pedal changes.

                    The switches under Include add a wah, an expression pedal, a looper, a Poly Capo (plays your guitar in another key without retuning) or a Stage boost. The DSP bar shows how much of the pedal's processing power is left: the pedal can only run so many effects at once, and the app keeps every preset within that limit.

                    3. Read a song

                    A song opens on its Play tab. The row of buttons across the top (Intro, Verse, Chorus, Solo) are the 4 snapshots: one sound for each part of the song. Tap one to see how the preset changes for that part. On the pedal you switch snapshots with footswitches FS1 and FS4.

                    Below them is the signal chain: each box is one block (an effect, the amp or the speaker cabinet), in the order your guitar's sound goes through them. A green lamp means the block is on in this snapshot, a dark lamp means off. "FS 2" on a block means footswitch 2 turns it on and off. Explain tells you what a block does and why it's there; Tune opens it big with sliders.

                    The other tabs: Tweak to change the sound with Claude, Notes for your own notes and the song plan (which pickup and knob settings to use in each part), and Versions for every earlier version of this song.

                    4. Change the sound

                    • In plain words: on the Tweak tab, describe what you want ("too muddy", "solo needs to jump out more", "less reverb in the verse") and tap Apply tweak. Claude makes a new version and lists every value it changed.
                    • By hand: tap Tune on a block, or Edit chain to add, move, swap or remove blocks. A bar at the bottom counts your changes. Save keeps them as a new version; Discard throws them away.
                    • Hear it as you go: turn on Live in that bottom bar and every change plays on the pedal straight away. Save writes it to the pedal; Discard puts the pedal back how it was.

                    Nothing is ever lost: each save or tweak is a new version, and the old one stays on the Versions tab. Star (Favorite) the one you like and the song opens on it next time. The guitar buttons under the song title switch to, or remake the song for, another of your guitars.

                    5. Send it to the pedal

                    1. Plug the pedal in with USB, switch it on, and close HX Edit.
                    2. Open the song and tap Send to pedal.
                    3. Pick a slot. Slots are the pedal's numbered preset spots: 1A, 1B, 1C, 1D, then 2A and so on. "Read preset names from pedal" shows what's already in each. Whatever was there is backed up first.
                    4. Tap Send to pedal. When it's done, the song list shows the slot next to the song, and the preset is ready to play.

                    No USB? Use the ⋯ menu > Download .hlx, then drag the file into HX Edit.

                    6. Set lists and Stage mode

                    On the Sets tab, tap + New set and + Add songs to make a set list in play order. A song can be in as many sets as you like. Send set to pedal puts every song in the set on the pedal, one slot after another, so you can step through them with the footswitches.

                    Stage (on a song, or a set) is a simple screen for gigs and rehearsal: big Prev and Next buttons, each pedal as an on/off button, and quick fixes like Louder, Quieter, Less boom or Gain boost that play on the pedal at once. When you leave a song, Keep saves your changes and Toss puts the pedal back. Lock stops stray taps while you play. The screen stays on while a song or Stage is open.

                    7. The Pedal tab (quick pedal control)

                    The Pedal tab works on whatever preset is on the pedal right now, even one you didn't make in this app. Step through the pedal's presets with ◀ ▶, tap Go live to tweak it with one-tap quick picks, or ask Claude for a change. Save as a song keeps it in your library.

                    8. Make it yours

                    • My tone > Tweaks for every song: things Claude should always do, like "cut some mud around 400 Hz" or "I play at bedroom volume". Tick "Remember this" on a tweak to save it here.
                    • Gear notes: how to handle one amp, effect or NAM capture whenever it's used.
                    • Quick picks: your own one-tap moves, like "Gig volume", shown when you tune a block.
                    • Suggested: ready-made starting points you can add with one tap.

                    Phones, other devices and backups

                    • Phones on this Wi-Fi (More): use the app from your phone or tablet at home. Turn it on, tap Pair a phone and scan the code with the phone's camera. The pedal stays plugged into this computer.
                    • Use pedal on this device (More, in Chrome): use a pedal plugged into the phone or laptop you're holding, for example an Android phone with a USB adapter. iPhones and iPads can't connect to the pedal.
                    • Back up or move your library (More): save every song, set and setting to one file, or move them to another computer.

                    Something isn't working

                    The pedal shows "not connected"
                    Check the pedal is switched on and the USB cable is plugged in at both ends. Quit HX Edit: while it's open the app can't reach the pedal. Try another USB port or cable. The pedal status at the top checks again every few seconds.
                    Sending to the pedal fails or times out
                    Most often this is the USB cable. An old cable can drop the pedal to a slow connection where reading works but sending doesn't. Swap in a newer cable. To check, open More > Report a problem and tap Test the pedal.
                    The pedal froze or stopped responding
                    Switch the pedal off, wait about ten seconds, and switch it back on. Then try again. Your presets are safe.
                    Setup says HX Edit isn't found
                    Install HX Edit from line6.com/software, even if you never open it. Then More > Set up this computer > Check again.
                    Generate preset doesn't work
                    Building with the AI needs an API key with credit on it, from Anthropic, OpenAI or Google. Add one in More > AI. Everything else works without one.
                    It sounds too loud, quiet, boomy or harsh
                    Tell Claude on the song's Tweak tab, or use Stage mode's quick fixes. Presets sound different on headphones, a small speaker and a PA, so set the output rig to match what you play through.
                    Still stuck?
                    More > Report a problem gathers what just happened so you can send it to the app's GitHub page. Personal details are blanked out first, and you can read it before sending.

                    What the words mean

                    Preset
                    One complete sound setup saved on the pedal: which amp and effects, and every knob setting.
                    Slot
                    A numbered spot on the pedal that holds one preset: 1A, 1B, 1C, 1D, 2A and so on.
                    Snapshot
                    A variation inside one preset, like Verse or Solo. Same amp and effects, with some turned on or off or set differently. Each song here has 4.
                    Block
                    One box in the chain: an effect pedal, the amp, the cabinet, and so on.
                    Signal chain
                    The blocks in the order your sound goes through them, from guitar to output.
                    Amp and cab
                    The amp model shapes the main tone. The cab (cabinet) is the speaker it plays through, which matters a lot on headphones or a PA.
                    Footswitch (FS)
                    The pedal's stomp switches. FS2, FS3, FS5 and FS6 turn effects on and off; FS1 and FS4 change snapshots.
                    DSP
                    The pedal's processing power. Each block uses some; when it runs out, nothing more fits.
                    Output rig
                    What the pedal plugs into: headphones, a speaker or PA, or a real amp. It decides whether the preset includes an amp.
                    FRFR
                    "Full range, flat response": a speaker made for modelers, which sounds like a PA rather than a guitar amp.
                    Expression pedal (EXP)
                    A rocker pedal for wah, volume or swells, plugged into the Stomp XL.
                    Wah
                    The "wah-wah" effect, worked with an expression pedal.
                    Looper
                    Records a part and plays it back while you play over it.
                    Poly Capo
                    Shifts your guitar up or down in pitch without retuning. It uses a lot of DSP.
                    Stage boost
                    An extra drive before the amp, off until you need more push. Stage mode's Gain boost turns it on.
                    Noise gate
                    Silences hiss and hum when you're not playing.
                    Version
                    One saved state of a song. Every tweak or save makes a new one, and the older ones are kept.
                    Fork
                    A new version made from an old one with a different guitar, rig or switches.
                    Live
                    Changes play on the pedal as you make them, before you save.
                    .hlx file
                    A preset saved as a file, the kind HX Edit imports and exports.
                    NAM and IR
                    NAM (Neural Amp Modeler) captures are recordings of real amps you can use instead of the pedal's amp models. An IR is a recording of a speaker cabinet. Both are optional and for advanced users.
                    API key
                    A password-like code from Anthropic, OpenAI or Google that lets this app use their AI, billed to your account.
