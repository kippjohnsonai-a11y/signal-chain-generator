# Signal chain order and block choice

Default order: tuner/utility -> dynamics (compressor) -> wah/filter -> pitch (whammy/octave) -> drives/fuzz -> noise gate (for high gain; or use the gate built into the input) -> modulation that should hit the amp (phaser/univibe/flanger for vintage tones) -> AMP+CAB -> EQ (post-amp shaping) -> modulation that should be clean (chorus) -> delay -> reverb -> volume pedal (if a volume swell/level pedal) -> looper.

- Fuzz (especially Fuzz Face / Tone Bender types) goes first, before wah, if the reference used it that way; otherwise wah before drive.
- Volume pedal before drives acts like the guitar volume knob (cleans up); after the amp it is a master volume. For swells put it after drives, before delay/reverb.
- Tight metal: a Tube Screamer-style drive (Scream 808) with Gain near 0-1.5/10, Level 7-8/10, Tone ~5 in front of a high-gain amp tightens the low end. Add a gate (Noise Gate or Horizon Gate) before the amp.
- Classic rock: amp does most of the gain; a mild overdrive boost for solos in a snapshot.
- Blues: low-to-medium gain amp; a Klon/TS style drive for pushed parts; spring or plate reverb.
- Ambient/clean: compressor, clean amp with headroom, chorus, stereo delays, big reverbs.
- Post-amp EQ ("Low and High Cut" or "Parametric") is the cleanest way to tame mud (low cut 80-120 Hz) and fizz (high cut 6-8 kHz).
- Prefer the model based on the gear used on the original recording when known. When the exact gear is unknown, choose by era and genre.
