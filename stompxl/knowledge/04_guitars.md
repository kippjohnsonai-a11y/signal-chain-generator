# Tailoring to the guitar

## Single-coil guitars (e.g. Fender Stratocaster, SSS)
- Lower output (~5-6 kOhm pickups): need roughly 10-25% more amp Drive or a drive pedal to reach the same saturation as humbuckers.
- Brighter and thinner: reduce Treble/Presence ~0.5-1.5 steps, raise Bass/Mid slightly; high cut 6.5-8 kHz for high-gain tones to avoid ice-pick.
- 60-cycle hum and noise: use a noise gate at higher gain (threshold around -55 to -45 dB); keep gain moderate.
- Pickup positions: neck = warm/round (blues leads, Hendrix/SRV cleans); positions 2 and 4 = "quack" (funk, Knopfler); bridge = thin and bright (needs body: more mids/bass, less treble). Recommend a position per snapshot.
- For heavy music that was recorded with humbuckers (e.g. Metallica), compensate: bridge pickup, more Drive, a TS-style boost with more Level, mids slightly up to avoid a thin scoop, and a tight gate.
- Guitar volume at 7-8 cleans up drives nicely - a useful live tip for Strat players.

## Humbucker guitars (e.g. Gibson Les Paul, HH)
- Higher output (~8-16 kOhm): drive the amp harder; use 10-25% less amp Drive than for single coils for the same saturation.
- Thicker and darker, more low-mids: raise low cut to 90-120 Hz on higher-gain tones, keep Bass moderate (4-5), Presence can go up a touch.
- Neck humbucker can get muddy with gain: suggest bridge for rhythm and neck for smooth leads with slightly lower drive and treble lifted.
- Quieter than single coils for hum, so gates can sit lower (around -60 dB) or be skipped at moderate gain.
- Les Paul + Marshall-style amps is the classic rock/hard-rock pairing; for glassy cleans humbuckers benefit from a brighter Fender-style amp with Bright on and treble up.

## General
- If the player's guitar differs from the one on the recording, state what you compensated for.
- Always give pickup selector and guitar volume/tone knob advice per snapshot.

## Tuning
- If the song is in a lower tuning than the player's guitar, the best answer is to retune the guitar: say so in the playing tips (e.g. "tune down a half step to Eb").
- Pitch blocks are a poor substitute for downtuning chords: Simple Pitch is monophonic and warbles on chords, and the Poly pitch blocks sound right but cost ~40% of the DSP. Only use a Poly pitch block for tuning when the rest of the chain is light, and never Simple Pitch for chord work.
