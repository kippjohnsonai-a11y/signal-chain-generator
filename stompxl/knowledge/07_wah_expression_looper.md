# Wah, expression pedal and looper

The player toggles which of these they want. Recommend them per song, but only include a block when its toggle is on.

## Wah (toggle "wah")
- Include when the recording uses wah (e.g. Hendrix, Kirk Hammett solos, Slash, funk rhythm), or for expressive solos if the player asked for it.
- Place before drives (or after fuzz for Fuzz Face-style rigs). Pick a model close to the original: Cry Baby = "Fassel", Vox V846 = "UK Wah 846", Dunlop 535Q = "Chrome"/"Chrome Custom", Teardrop 310 for Kirk Hammett style.
- Its "Pedal" parameter goes on EXP 1. The block is off in snapshots where the wah is not used and on in the snapshot where it is (typically SOLO); it also gets a footswitch. With a Mission SP1-L6H pedal the toe switch flips between EXP 1 (wah) and EXP 2 (volume) and engages the right block.

## Expression pedal (toggle "expression")
- The player's pedal setup is stated with the request. With ONE ordinary pedal (e.g. a Moog EP-3, the default), a preset can hold only one swept block - a wah, a Volume pedal, or a Pitch Wham - all on EXP 1. Pick the one the song needs most: wah for wah songs, otherwise a Volume pedal for swells, or Pitch Wham for whammy parts.
- EXP 1: wah sweep, or Pitch Wham (whammy dives, e.g. Tom Morello, Dimebag) when the song calls for it.
- Default use of the pedal when the song doesn't need a wah or whammy: a 'Volume' block, placed after the amp/cab and before delay/reverb, so swells and fades don't change the amp's gain and delay/reverb tails keep ringing.
- Ambient / post-rock: volume swells into long delay and reverb.
- If there's no wah and no clear use, recommend a volume pedal after the drives.

## Looper (toggle "looper")
- Add the "One Switch Looper" block as the LAST block so recorded loops keep their tone when snapshots change.
- Recommend it for practising solos over a rhythm part, songwriting, or songs built on repeating riffs.
- It costs one of the 8 blocks: drop a non-essential block rather than an amp, drive or core time-based effect.

## Poly Capo

- The Poly Capo toggle adds one Poly Capo block: a polyphonic pitch shift (Interval in semitones, -24 to +24) that lets the player match a song recorded with a capo or in another tuning without retuning or clamping a real capo. Chords track cleanly, unlike Simple Pitch.
- Place it first or right after the wah, before drives, compressors and the amp: pitch tracking works best on the clean guitar signal. Keep it on in every snapshot with Mix at 100%; Tracking "X Stable" for chords, "Fast" for quick single-note lines.
- It costs about 40% of the DSP. With an amp and cab (~38%) that leaves roughly 20%: a drive, a delay and a standard reverb, or a compressor, a drive and a delay, or one premium reverb on its own. Never a second Poly block, and no Pitch Wham alongside it unless the rig has no amp/cab.
